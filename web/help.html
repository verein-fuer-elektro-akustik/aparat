    getting started

    aparat is a modular synthesizer: sound comes from modules, and cables between their jacks decide what goes where. Nothing is fixed, any output can go to any input.

    1. Press start in the header. Browsers only play sound after a click, so aparat stays silent until then.
    2. Play the computer keyboard: the keys a w s e d f t g … play notes, z and x move an octave down and up. A midi keyboard works too, see midi.
    3. Turn a knob: drag it up or down. Try cutoff on the vcf while a note plays.
    4. Add a module: + module, or shift+a at the pointer. Type to search; the menu describes the module in focus.
    5. Patch a cable: drag from a jack to another jack. Outputs are the filled jacks at the bottom of a panel, inputs the rings above them.

    The patch menu (the patch name at the top left) holds example patches: berlin plays a sequence by itself, acid, idm and drones show other ways of patching, ms-50 is laid out like the Korg MS-50.

    signals and voltages

    Every cable carries one value per sample, in volts, the way hardware modular synthesizers do (aparat follows the conventions of VCV Rack). The same cable can carry sound or control; what it means depends on the input it goes to.

    audio
    about ±5 V. The output module turns 5 V into full scale.
    pitch
    1 V per octave: one volt up is one octave up, 1/12 V one semitone. 0 V is middle c (261.63 hz) on a vco with tune at 0.
    gates and triggers
    10 V while on, 0 V while off. Inputs read anything above 1 V as on. A gate stays on as long as a key is held; a trigger is a short pulse.
    envelopes and cv
    envelopes go from 0 to 10 V, lfos swing ±5 V. A knob labelled cv or fm sets how far the voltage at its input moves the knob's value.

    An input with nothing patched uses a fixed value, usually 0 V. Some use another one: the vca's cv input is 10 V, so an unpatched vca is simply open.

    Each cable delays its signal by one sample. That makes feedback possible: a cable may lead back to an earlier module, for example a delay's output into its own input through a filter.

    Chords need one voice per note: one midi in module per voice, with of set to the number of voices and voice to 1, 2, 3 … on each, then a vco, filter and envelope behind every midi in.

    modules and cables

    modules

    • Move a module by dragging its title. Neighbours make room; esc during the drag puts everything back.
    • Right-click a module for a colour: one of the swatches, any colour with +, or none. It colours all the selected modules and is kept in the patch. Cables take the colour of the module their signal comes from; from a module without one, gates and pitch have colours of their own, the rest is grey.
    • Click a module to select it, shift-click to select several, or shift-drag on empty space to draw a box around them. Several selected modules move together.
    • Right-click a module for random (random knob values), reset, duplicate, copy, remove and help, which opens its entry on this page.
    • Effects and other modules that shape a signal have an on button, lit while the module runs. Switch it off to bypass the module: the signal then goes straight through, as if the module were not there.
    • Drop an audio file on empty rack space for a sampler that plays it, or on a sampler to load it there.

    knobs and buttons

    • Drag a knob up or down, or turn the mouse wheel over it; hold shift for fine steps. The value shows while the pointer is over it. Faders (the mixer's) work the same, their cap follows the pointer.
    • Double-click a knob to reset it. When a knob has focus, the arrow keys move it.
    • Limit a knob or fader to part of its travel: ctrl+drag it (cmd on a Mac). On a knob the left half moves the lowest point and the right half the highest; on a fader, above or below the middle of the range. The range shows as an arc or a stretch of the track, and turning, the wheel, random and midi controllers stay inside it (a controller's whole travel spans the range). ctrl+double-click takes that limit away.
    • Buttons have their name on them and are filled while on; a click switches them. The button module's push is on only while it is held.
    • Right-click a knob or button for its menu: midi learn (on buttons, a key or a controller, see midi), forget a link it has, reset it, clear its limits; below come the module's items. shift+right-click learns at once, without the menu.

    cables

    • Drag from one jack to another to patch a cable. An output can feed any number of inputs; an input takes one cable.
    • Pull a cable off a jack to move it to another jack, or drop it on empty space to remove it.
    • ctrl-drag from a jack that already has a cable for another one.
    • Click a cable to select it, delete removes it; delete also removes the cable under the pointer. Hovering a jack lights its cables in colour and fades the others.
    • How far cables hang is set with the cables slider in the view menu; the cables follow as it moves, a double-click on it gives the usual slack back. It is kept in this browser, not in the patch.
    • Jacks for gates and triggers are a rounded square, jacks for pitch (1 V/oct) have a dot in the middle; holding the pointer over a jack names its kind. It is only a hint: any output can go into any input, and many great patches do exactly that.
    • Outputs that carry events (gates, triggers, clocks, and the gamepad and pad) light up while they are high, so you can see them fire with no cable patched.
    • Cables glow with the level of their signal while audio runs.

    the view

    • The rack goes on in every direction. Drag empty space or a panel (away from its knobs and jacks), middle-drag anywhere, or scroll (shift+wheel sideways, two fingers on a trackpad) to move the view. On a touch screen, drag with one finger, also over a panel away from its knobs and jacks, and pinch to zoom.
    • ctrl+wheel, a pinch, or + − 0 zoom the rack around the pointer (the buttons in the view menu zoom around the middle).
    • 1, or show the whole patch in the view menu, zooms and moves the view so the whole patch fits, when you have lost it.
    • While audio runs, the bar at the top fades away after a few seconds and the rack takes its space; move the pointer to the top edge to get it back. It stays while recording. The view menu switches this off.
    • theme in the view menu: light, dark, or as the system is set (the default). The choice is kept in this browser.

    composites

    A composite is a module made of modules: a voice of vco, filter, envelope and vca, an effect chain, a sequencer setup. It sits in the rack as one panel and is used like any other module, as often as you like.

    • Select the modules (shift-click or shift-drag), right-click one of them and choose make composite.
    • Give it a name and tick the knobs it should show on its panel. Cables between the chosen modules stay inside; cables to other modules become the composite's jacks, named after the jacks they lead to.
    • The composite then appears at the top of the add-module menu, to add it again. Each one has its own knob settings and its own sound.
    • A composite is marked by two stacked squares after its name. To change the inside, click them, double-click the composite's title, or right-click it and choose edit composite. Its inside opens in a view of its own, with the path at the top (patch › voice); click done, a name in the path, or press esc to go back. The patch keeps playing while you edit, and every composite of that name follows the changes as you make them.
    • Inside, the panels in and out are the composite's jacks. Patch from the + jack of in (or to the + of out) for a new jack; a jack without cables inside goes away.
    • panel, at the top of the edit view, names the jacks, chooses the knobs on the composite's panel and their names, and gives it a description for the add-module menu. Cables and midi links follow a rename.
    • A knob turned inside changes every composite of that name, unless the knob is on the composite's panel: then each one keeps its own setting.
    • ungroup composite turns one composite back into its modules, in the patch.
    • Composites are kept in the patch, so they travel with it: stored patches, files, links and copied modules all carry them along. A composite can contain composites.

    patches

    A patch is the modules, their knobs and the cables. The patch menu is the first in the header and is named after the loaded patch; a * after the name means it changed since it was loaded or stored. Click the name to open the menu, which has:

    new patch
    a small starting patch: midi in, vco, vcf, envelope, vca, scope and output.
    open… and save
    load a patch from a file in place of this one, and save this one as a file. A patch file dropped anywhere on the window opens it too.
    import…
    add the modules of a patch file to this patch, with their settings and the cables between them. They land in new rows below the rack, selected, so they can be moved together. Inside a composite they go into the composite.
    copy link
    copies a link that holds the whole patch. Nothing is stored on the server; whoever opens the link gets the patch.
    stored patches
    the patches on the server. Type a name and store (or ctrl+s); load and delete work on the named patch. A stored patch has its own address, …/aparat/patch/name. The example patches come with aparat: storing over one keeps your version, deleting that brings the original back.

    Unsaved changes are kept in the browser and come back on the next visit, so a closed tab loses nothing. The same goes for copied modules and the audio files of samplers: they stay in this browser. A patch only remembers the names of its audio files, so on another computer they have to be loaded again.

    ctrl+z undoes, ctrl+shift+z redoes (also in the edit menu).

    audio and recording

    The header shows the number of output channels, the sample rate, the latency, how much of the audio time the patch uses, and a lamp per output channel that lights when it clips.

    output
    the sound card. Device names show after show device names, which asks for microphone permission (the browser only names devices then).
    sample rate
    the sound card's rate, or one the browser converts to it. Higher rates sound cleaner and need more cpu; low ones sound duller, like an old sampler, and save cpu.
    latency
    low, balanced, safe, or a time in ms. A larger buffer helps when the sound crackles. The browser picks the nearest buffer it can do; the header shows what you got.

    The output module sends its inputs to the sound card's channels from first on. aparat uses every channel the browser offers. For more than two, set the audio interface up as multichannel in the system's sound settings; to play on several devices at once, combine them there (an aggregate device on macOS, a combined sink with PipeWire on Linux).

    The input module brings the sound card's inputs in; the browser asks for microphone access when it is added.

    record records what goes to the sound card as a wav file, until it is pressed again.

    midi

    • enable midi in the midi menu asks the browser for midi access, then in and out choose the ports.
    • The midi in module turns notes into pitch, gate and velocity; the computer keyboard plays into it too. Further modules read controllers (midi cc in) and clock (midi clock in), or send notes, controllers and clock out.
    • Midi learn: right-click a knob, choose midi learn, then move a controller; the knob follows it from then on. forget cc … in the same menu removes the link. shift+right-click learns without the menu, twice removes the link. The links are kept with the patch.

    controllers

    • pad is a touch surface: drag on it with the mouse, a pen or a finger for x and y. With a pen or a finger, press follows how hard you press. The position is kept with the patch.
    • Key learn: right-click a button (a button module's push, a clock's run, an effect's on, a mixer's mute or solo…), choose learn a key or controller, then press a key. The key then holds the button down while you hold it, or flips the switch each time you press it, and shows on the panel. forget key … in its menu removes it (or shift+right-click twice), esc stops learning. Keys with a job of their own (delete, esc, the zoom keys) can't be learned; a playing key that is learned plays the button instead of its note. Knobs learn midi controllers only. The links are kept with the patch.
    • camera turns the camera into voltages: how light the picture is, how much moves and where, and the motion in each of nine zones, so you can play by waving in front of it. The browser asks for the camera the first time. It only runs while audio is on and a camera module is in the patch; the picture stays in your browser, nothing is sent anywhere.
    • portal is a wireless cable: what goes into a portal comes out of every other portal on the same channel, in this patch or in aparat open in another tab of this browser. Use it to run several patches from one clock. The panel shows how many other portals are linked. It is for gates, clocks and control voltages; changes take a few ms, and each tab's sound card clock drifts a little, so it can't carry audio.
    • gamepad reads a game controller plugged in or paired with the computer. Browsers only show a controller after one of its buttons was pressed, so press one first. Sticks, triggers, buttons and the d-pad each have a jack; pad picks the controller when there are several.

    modules

    Every module with what it does, its inputs, its outputs and its knobs. An input's value in brackets is what it uses with nothing patched, when that is not 0 V.
